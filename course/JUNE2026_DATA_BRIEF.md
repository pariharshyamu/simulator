## Headline figures — use these exact numbers

- Total thermal energy sent out, June 2026: **4,588.47 MU**; net bill **Rs 2,806.55 crore**.
- Combined net heat-rate gap against MERC norms across the 13 thermal groups: **Rs 56 crore in June 2026 alone** (approximately Rs 676 crore a year if the month is typical).
- Auxiliary consumption above norm: **82 MU in one month**, worth **Rs 33 crore** at each station's own variable charge. That is roughly the annual output of a small unit, consumed inside the fence.
- Fixed-charge disallowance for availability shortfall: **Rs 100.87 crore cumulative**, of which **Rs 32.93 crore** was adjusted in the June 2026 bill.
- Provisional Fuel Surcharge Adjustment for June 2026: **minus Rs 101.44 crore** (a withdrawal), because most stations' actual ECR ran below the rate billed. Nashik and Uran were the exceptions and carried a positive FSA.

## Additional verified detail

- Nashik Units 3-5 is the **most expensive coal station in the MERC merit order stack** at Rs 5.98/kWh, ranked 10th overall in the MSEDCL stack and behind only gas stations. It also has the **highest availability in the fleet at 94.62 %** and the lowest PLF among the high-availability stations at 58.40 %. The station is available and is not being called.
- Nashik's **gross heat rate of 2,440 kcal/kWh is 17 kcal/kWh BETTER than its normative 2,458**. The entire net heat-rate penalty of 50 kcal/kWh comes from auxiliary consumption at 12.96 % against a norm of 10.75 %.
- Parli Unit 8 has the fleet's highest availability at **97.40 %**; Khaperkheda Units 1-4 the lowest at **57.85 %**.
- As-fired GCV ranges from **2,733 kcal/kg (Khaperkheda 5) to 3,270 kcal/kg (Parli 8)**. Stacking loss is taken at 85 kcal/kg for all stations.
- Implied as-fired coal cost ranges from **Rs 3,655/tonne (Khaperkheda 5) to Rs 6,217/tonne (Nashik)** — a 70 % spread across one company's own fleet.
- Khaperkheda 1-4, Khaperkheda 5, Nashik, Chandrapur 3-7, Chandrapur 8-9 and Koradi 8-10 burn **imported coal** at 4,624 to 4,709 kcal/kg as-fired, blended with domestic and washed coal.
- Bhusawal 4-5 and Bhusawal 6 are **co-firing biomass pellets** (3,210 and 3,271 kcal/kg as fired respectively); Khaperkheda 1-4 shows a biomass entry at 2,950 kcal/kg.
- FGD auxiliary consumption is essentially **zero everywhere except Khaperkheda 1-4 (0.02 %) and Koradi 6 (0.03 %)** — FGD load is still to come across most of the fleet.
